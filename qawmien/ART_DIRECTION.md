# Art direction: high fantasy, with Malta hidden in it

**DECIDED 2026-09-04, and it reverses what this file used to say.** The owner:
*"fantasy world I want, no Maltese — little easter eggs Maltese."*

So the island is a **fantasy world first**. Lush, storybook, the kind of place
a beginner island in this genre is: green country, woods, a river, a village
you want to walk into. It is not Malta made strange, which is what the whole
of this document used to argue for.

Malta stays as **easter eggs** — deliberate, sparse, and a delight when you
spot one rather than the identity of the place. A luzzu drawn up on the shore
with the Eye of Osiris on its prow. One stretch of dry-stone wall where a
fence would do. A gallarija on the tavern. A name here and there. Somebody
Maltese should grin twice an hour, not feel they are being sold their own
island back.

WHY THIS IS WRITTEN DOWN RATHER THAN JUST DONE: every art brief in the repo
carried the old direction, so a generation asked for today would still have
come back arid and silver-grey. tools/paintjob.py is updated with it, and so
is the palette below. The old text is kept underneath, because the reasoning
in it was sound and the easter eggs are drawn FROM it.

| | |
|---|---|
| green | **lush** — meadow, moss, deep woodland shade. The opposite of the old note |
| earth | warm brown loam; red-brown clay on paths and tilled fields |

The mistake to avoid now is the opposite of the old one: **do not make it
arid**. Silver-grey scrub, prickly pear and bare rock everywhere is the look
this direction replaces.

# The old direction, kept for the reasoning and the easter eggs

The island is **Malta made strange** — not generic fantasy with Maltese names
stuck on, and emphatically not Amakna. Every zone should be recognisable to
someone who has stood on the real island, and impossible on it.

This matters commercially as much as artistically: the game is *Il-Qawmien*,
it ships inside KARTI, a Maltese app, for Maltese players. A world they
recognise is the one thing no competitor can copy.

## The palette

Malta's colour is **honey limestone** — globigerina, which goes from pale cream
to gold as it weathers. That is the ground note of the whole island.

| | |
|---|---|
| stone | honey, cream, weathered gold; grey where it is old and wet |
| earth | red-brown terra rossa, the soil that collects in the field walls |
| green | **dusty, silver-grey** — carob, olive, prickly pear. Never lush. Malta is dry |
| sea | deep blue shading to turquoise over sand, and it is BRIGHT |
| accent | the blue-and-yellow of a luzzu, and the Eye of Osiris painted on its prow |

The most common mistake to avoid: **English-meadow green**. This island is arid.
The green is sparse, silvery and grows out of stone.

## Materials

- **Dry stone walls** (*ħitan tas-sejjieħ*) — the field boundaries, laid without
  mortar. These should be everywhere, and they are what makes the terraces read
  as Maltese rather than as generic fields.
- **Limestone slabs**, cart-rutted and sun-bleached.
- **Salt pans** — shallow rectangles cut *into* the rock at the shore, holding
  still water and white crusts. Geometric, man-made, unmistakable.
- **Domes and bell-gables** on buildings; flat roofs, not pitched.
- **Wooden balconies** (*gallarija*), closed and painted, jutting from upper
  floors. One of these on the tavern would place the whole village instantly.

## The fantasy, and where it comes from

Malta's own history hands us the fantasy without inventing much:

- **The megalithic temples** — Ġgantija means *the giantess*: the folklore is
  that a giant woman built them. So the ruin the player wakes in is not a
  generic dungeon; it is a temple raised by something far larger than a person,
  and the scale should say so.
- **The catacombs** — miles of real rock-cut burial tunnels under the island.
  That is the Necropolis, and it does not need embellishing.
- **The watchtowers** — squat coastal towers built to watch for raiders. One on
  the horizon of a coastal screen is instant place and instant story.
- **A collapsed stone arch** in the sea, remembered but no longer standing —
  the Azure Window fell in 2017. A *fantasy* Malta could still have it, or have
  its stump, and locals would feel that.
- **Luzzu boats** with eyes painted on the prow to ward off harm.

The floating island is the one openly unreal thing, and it should feel like the
real island *lifted* — the sea below, the coast raw where it tore away.

## What this is not

- Not Amakna. No mushroom-cute, no cartoon saturation.
- Not northern-European fantasy. No pine forests, no thatched cottages, no fog.
- Not tourist-brochure Malta either — this is the island as somewhere people
  *work* and are *buried*, with something older underneath.

## Originality

The STRUCTURE is learned from Incarnam — a small floating beginner island of
about 8x9 screens, zones with their own monster families, a dungeon in the
corner. That is layout knowledge and it is fine to use.

The ART is ours and is generated from **our own blockout templates**, never
from anyone else's map image. Fantasy Malta is not a reskin of Amakna; it is a
different island that happens to be organised as sensibly.
